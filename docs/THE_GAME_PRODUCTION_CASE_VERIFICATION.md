## Production system

John Brancato and Michael Ferris created the original screenplay, while Andrew Kevin Walker later confirmed extensive uncredited rewriting. David Fincher and Propaganda Films producer Steve Golin organise the premise as an engineered loss of control: Consumer Recreation Services converts the protagonist's normal social and physical world into a performed reality whose actors, objects, spaces and media cannot be separated reliably from ordinary life.

The coordinated system combines:

- Michael Douglas's established corporate-authority persona and its progressive physical and interpretive collapse;
- a restricted viewpoint that keeps the audience inside Nicholas Van Orton's uncertainty;
- Propaganda Films and PolyGram Filmed Entertainment production and distribution structures;
- almost entirely real San Francisco and Mexicali location work, with additional Los Angeles stage production;
- Filoli as the inherited mansion, temporarily transformed with black-light graffiti;
- a modern CRS office built inside a functioning San Francisco workplace;
- Jeffrey Beecroft's contrast between inherited wealth, corporate abstraction and increasingly rewritable urban space;
- Michael Kaplan's tailored class identity and gradual dishevelment;
- Harris Savides's shadowed Panavision image using GII and Platinum cameras and a concentrated 27 mm, 40 mm and 85 mm Primo lens set;
- extensive practical lighting systems, including UV fixtures, rotating process light, bus bounce and a crane-suspended forty-foot moon box;
- VistaVision process plates, rear projection, vehicle rigs, practical stunts and integrated digital effects;
- James Haygood's information control, which preserves local clarity while withholding the larger causal system;
- Ren Klyce's 5.1 field of urban clamor, mediated voices, alarms, vehicles and controlled quiet;
- Howard Shore's atmospheric score and the mansion sequence's use of Jefferson Airplane's White Rabbit;
- a production principle in which every technical department must plausibly belong both to the real city and to CRS's hidden performance.

## Existing family

The resolver family is `subjective_enclosure_performance` because the film encloses Nicholas inside a single unstable interpretation of events while nearly every person around him may be performing a second identity. The enclosure is not a single room: it is a city-wide production system that turns architecture, service labour, media, danger and social contact into staged evidence.
