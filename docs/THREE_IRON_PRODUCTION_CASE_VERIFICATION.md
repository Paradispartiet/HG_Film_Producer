## Production system

3-Iron is treated as a **silent empty-house occupation-romance system**, not as a generic romantic drama or a purely symbolic ghost story.

The playable model connects:

- the international expansion of South Korean auteur cinema in the early 2000s;
- Kim Ki-duk as writer, director, producer and editor inside a compact South Korean-Japanese production;
- advertising flyers as a practical method for identifying empty homes;
- entry without theft, followed by laundry, repairs and photographs beside strangers' possessions;
- real furnished houses and the objects already inside them as the material design system;
- Jae Hee and Lee Seung-yeon's near-silent performance, shared labour and resistance to abusive ownership;
- Jang Seung-beck's composed colour 35 mm domestic frames, thresholds and negative space;
- golf balls and the 3-iron moving from play and warning into violence and consequence;
- doors, locks, clocks, scales, laundry, footsteps and impacts carrying information normally assigned to dialogue;
- Kim's editing of repeated occupation rituals and the prison transition into impossible physical evasion;
- Slvian's restrained music inside a predominantly practical acoustic world;
- the final return making love, possession, visibility and reality deliberately impossible to separate.
