# Queen Elizabeth (1912) – Production Case verification

## Canonical identity

- Scenario ID: `scenario_queen_elizabeth_1912`
- Original title: *Les Amours de la reine Élisabeth*
- Display title: *Queen Elizabeth*
- Year: 1912
- Production company: Le Film d'Art
- Directors in the Pathé record: Louis Mercanton, Henri Desfontaines, Gaston Roudès
- Scenario runtime model: 47 minutes, following the Fondation Jérôme Seydoux-Pathé restoration listing
- Chapter role: Film History Chapter 4 P1 case for prestige performance, imported features and distribution entrepreneurship
- Verification date: 2026-08-14

## What is verified

1. **French Film d'Art production.** Fondation Jérôme Seydoux-Pathé lists Le Film d'Art as production company and identifies the 1912 film as a 47-minute French work.
2. **Prestige adaptation.** Émile Moreau's play, Sarah Bernhardt's Queen Elizabeth and Lou Tellegen's Essex form the documented dramatic and performance core.
3. **Credits with an explicit cataloguing boundary.** Pathé credits Mercanton, Desfontaines and Roudès, while MoMA and NYPL use narrower attributions. The Atlas follows the fuller Pathé record and documents the variation instead of hiding it.
4. **Clément-Maurice photography.** Pathé identifies the cinematographer; gameplay uses this to justify source-backed visual authorship without inventing an undocumented modern coverage plan.
5. **Paul Poiret costumes.** BFI and NYPL identify Poiret. BFI describes the designs as stylized Renaissance-inflected couture rather than strict Elizabethan reconstruction.
6. **United States rights and presentation.** BFI and NYPL connect Adolph Zukor to the American rights, financing and presentation. The Production Case keeps those functions separate from French production authorship.
7. **Feature-market importance.** BFI and MoMA describe the release as an important success in changing assumptions about prestige, stars and longer films.
8. **Silent production versus accompaniment.** MoMA describes a silent 35 mm print with musical accompaniment. Accompaniment remains an exhibition layer, not synchronized original production sound.
9. **Version and runtime differences.** Institutional records describe 47-minute, 45-minute and 36-minute manifestations. These are treated as explicit print/version evidence rather than collapsed into one asserted complete original cut.

## Film Study coverage

The case covers all 17 Film Study areas. Directly evidenced areas are `source_verified`; areas where the surviving institutional record does not justify exact reconstruction remain `mapped` or `not_central`.

## Historiographic safeguards

The case does **not** claim that:

- Queen Elizabeth invented feature cinema;
- Sarah Bernhardt invented screen acting;
- Adolph Zukor directed or solely produced the French film;
- one modern print is the uniquely complete original version;
- modern accompaniment is synchronized original production sound;
- Poiret's designs are strict Elizabethan reconstruction.

Instead, the film is taught as a particularly consequential meeting of French prestige production, an international stage star, multi-reel duration, couture and American distribution entrepreneurship.
