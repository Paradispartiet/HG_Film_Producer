## What is verified

The Production Case is built from institutional film-history and archive records:

1. **Four-reel feature scale.** Harvard Film Archive documents Adolph Zukor's July 1912 import of the four-reel French Sarah Bernhardt film.
2. **Film d'Art production.** Fondation Jérôme Seydoux-Pathé identifies Le Film d'Art and provides production credits including Émile Moreau and Clément-Maurice.
3. **Sarah Bernhardt as a production and marketing asset.** The case treats her established theatrical celebrity as part of the film's prestige strategy rather than as a generic casting fact.
4. **35mm material.** Fondation Jérôme Seydoux-Pathé and MoMA both document 35mm material/presentation.
5. **Transatlantic rights and exhibition.** PBS American Experience documents Zukor and Daniel Frohman's U.S. rights acquisition, the Lyceum Theatre prestige premiere, the profitable tour and the subsequent Famous Players venture.
6. **Feature-business significance.** Harvard and MoMA connect the film's success to wider acceptance of longer productions and feature-oriented strategy.
7. **Runtime variability.** Fondation Jérôme Seydoux-Pathé lists a 47-minute restored presentation while MoMA lists a 36-minute presentation. The Atlas therefore uses 47 minutes as a playable restored-copy model but does not claim one universal original projection runtime.
8. **Silent production versus accompaniment.** MoMA identifies a silent film with musical accompaniment in presentation. The case does not turn venue accompaniment into synchronized original production sound.

## Historiography safeguards

The Production Case permanently avoids four misleading shortcuts:

- It does **not** claim that *Queen Elizabeth* invented the feature film.
- It does **not** claim that Sarah Bernhardt invented the screen star system.
- It does **not** treat one modern runtime as the uniquely correct 1912 projection duration.
- It does **not** describe live or venue-specific musical accompaniment as synchronized original production sound.

Instead, the case teaches *Queen Elizabeth* as a transatlantic prestige catalyst inside a broader international feature transition.
