# Employees Leaving Brown's Atlas Works, Sheffield (1901) — Production Case Verification

## Canonical case

- Scenario: `scenario_employees_leaving_browns_atlas_works_sheffield_1901`
- Chapter: Film History 2 — **Projection, programmes and audiences**
- Priority: **P1 Comparative Film**
- Makers: **Sagar Mitchell and James Kenyon**
- Runtime model: **2 minutes**
- Production type: local nonfiction / factory-gate / showman-commissioned actuality

## Why this is a distinct Production Case

This case is not merely a British repetition of *Workers Leaving the Lumière Factory*. Its chapter value is the commercial feedback loop between **subject, production and audience**:

1. a known Sheffield workplace provides a recognizable local setting;
2. the crowd is actively marshalled past the camera rather than neutrally observed;
3. recognizable workers and children make the film attractive to their own community;
4. Mitchell and Kenyon's broader business was tied to fairground operators and other showmen who paid for local films;
5. the filmed community could later become the paying audience.

The playable problem is therefore how to produce a local actuality **for people who expect to recognize themselves, neighbours and workplaces when the film is projected back to them**.

## Source pack

### British Film Institute — exact film

`https://player.bfi.org.uk/free/film/watch-employees-leaving-browns-atlas-works-sheffield-1901-1901-online`

Controls the film-specific evidence: 1901 Sheffield, approximately two minutes, the Atlas Works crowd, active marshaling/staging and the concluding workers/children acknowledging the camera. BFI treats the smartly dressed man as a possible link to commissioner A. D. Thomas, not a certain identification.

### BFI National Archive — Mitchell & Kenyon collection

`https://replay.bfi.org.uk/collection/430`

Provides the business context. Mitchell and Kenyon travelled through Britain making films for fairground operators and other showmen to screen to paying audiences; local recognition was a central attraction.

### Sheffield Libraries / Sheffield City Archives

`https://shefflibraries.blogspot.com/2021/07/introducing-our-writer-in-residence.html`

Provides an independent local archival use of the surviving film as evidence of Sheffield's social history and confirms the continuing local significance of the Atlas Works footage.

### National Science and Media Museum

`https://blog.scienceandmediamuseum.org.uk/very-short-history-of-cinema/`

Supplies broader exhibition context for early silent films shown through fairgrounds, music halls and other venues before dedicated cinema became dominant. It supports the exhibition layer rather than film-specific authorship.

## Permanent safeguards

- **No actuality-equals-transparent-reality shortcut.** BFI explicitly records crowd marshaling/staging.
- **No certainty inflation around A. D. Thomas.** The possible identification remains uncertain.
- **No forced modern auteur model.** Mitchell and Kenyon are the canonical makers, but the surviving record does not justify inventing a precise modern director/camera division for this individual film.
- **No synchronized-sound fiction.** The photographed production is silent; any live music, lecture or audience response belongs to a variable exhibition layer.
- **No generic factory-gate duplication.** This case earns Atlas space because local recognition and later paid exhibition are part of the production strategy.
