## Production argument

Cristian Mungiu builds the film from one concealed personal abortion account and a wider field of testimony about reproductive control under Ceaușescu. The screenplay narrows a longer first draft into one day of practical tasks and moves Otilia into the central viewpoint. Dialogue remains open to revision once locations and actors are known, but performance is then rehearsed with exact wording, pauses, dropped letters and low-volume everyday delivery.

The production uses real dormitory, hotel, street and apartment locations. Mihaela Poenaru's material reconstruction keeps late-1980s scarcity in buses, cigarettes, cars, dark streets and ordinary objects rather than decorative historical landmarks. Oleg Mutu and Mungiu reject tripod, Steadicam, dolly and crane systems, usually staging one shot per scene and allowing actors and dialogue to continue beyond the frame. The camera becomes mobile when Otilia is mobile and remains still when she is trapped in static social situations.

Dana Bunescu's editing protects sustained procedural scenes and uses ellipsis between tasks rather than coverage-driven acceleration. Her sound work reconstructs difficult passages such as the dinner scene from syllables, fragments and foley. Mungiu's explicit rejection of explanatory music leaves speech, traffic, rooms, footsteps, silence and unseen activity to carry suspense.
