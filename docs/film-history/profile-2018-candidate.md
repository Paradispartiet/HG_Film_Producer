# Profile (2018) — candidate study

Chapter 30 · P2 · Not a canonical production case.

Profile is a screen-based fictional thriller adapted from Anna Érelle's nonfiction account of a journalist investigating online extremist recruitment. The filmmakers discuss the adaptation and the lead actor's research in interviews. The film is not a documentary transcript. Its useful teaching focus is screen performance and the distinction between evidence and dramatization; exercises use invented ordinary interactions rather than real recruitment material or impersonation. ([moveable](https://moveablefest.com/timur-bekmambetov-profile/), [kane](https://www.blackfilmandtv.com/exclusives/exclusive-valene-kane-on-filming-timur-bekmambetovs-new-screenlife-film-profile), [release](https://www.screendaily.com/news/focus-features-takes-world-on-timur-bekmambetov-isis-thriller-profile/5158344.article))

Unlike the largely post-built desktop in Unfriended, Bekmambetov prepared a digital environment for interaction during performance, retaining control of individual screen elements for later changes. The approach brings production design and acting into the same workflow. The film's credit records identify Andrey Shugaev as editor and Ben Smith as production designer. The available testimony describes capabilities, not the proprietary implementation or exact recording specifications. ([moveable](https://moveablefest.com/timur-bekmambetov-profile/), [credits](https://cineuropa.org/en/film/346974/))

## Six-phase practice

- **Adaptation and research ethics:** Make a source ledger distinguishing the published account, the adaptation and an invented everyday scene.
- **Digital production design:** Design a local fictional desktop with three harmless interactive props and no network connection.
- **Rehearsal and distributed performance:** Rehearse an agreed fictional conversation between two performers using a private local mock interface.
- **Editable capture and editorial:** Make a layered mock sequence with separate performance, graphics and timing notes.
- **Sound as performance and post:** Plan two original interface sounds, one music-level change and a separate dialogue review.
- **Release and version provenance:** Prepare a version ledger with the festival record, later release, running-time discrepancy and rights check.

No real-world recruitment contact, impersonation or approved representation gap is part of this study.
