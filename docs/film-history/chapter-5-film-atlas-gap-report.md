# Film History Chapter 5 – Film Atlas gap report

## Scope

**Chapter 5: Cinema becomes international (1907–1914)**

This audit treats prewar cinema as a transnational production and circulation system rather than a sequence of isolated national inventions. France, Italy, Denmark, the United States and other production centres competed through company networks, stars, genres, rights sales, translated intertitles and increasingly long films.

Canonical Atlas baseline at the audit: **394 playable films**.

## Decision matrix

1. **Queen Elizabeth (1912)** / *La Reine Elisabeth* → `scenario_queen_elizabeth_1912`
   - Reused for French prestige production, Sarah Bernhardt's transatlantic star value and the United States import-rights business around longer films.

2. **Traffic in Souls (1913)** → `scenario_traffic_in_souls_1913`
   - Reused as the American independent-feature comparison: IMP production, Universal distribution and feature economics developing under the same international market pressure.

### P0 – must be produced

1. **Fantômas (1913)**
   - France/Gaumont anchor.
   - Production Case function: recurring popular-fiction adaptation, company-scale genre production, serial continuity, René Navarre's screen identity and international circulation.
   - This is not a claim that serial cinema began with *Fantômas*; it is the strongest production case for the chapter's Gaumont/popular-genre axis.

2. **Cabiria (1914)**
   - Italy/export-spectacle anchor.
   - Production Case function: large-scale historical mise-en-scène, Turin production infrastructure, prestige authorship, music, feature length and worldwide circulation.
   - The case must preserve the difference between the reconstructed 1914 version and Pastrone's later 1931 sound reworking.

### P1 – should be produced

1. **Afgrunden (1910)** / *The Abyss*
   - Danish star-system and performance case.
   - Production Case function: Asta Nielsen's film breakthrough, screen-specific performance, erotic melodrama and the way a star identity could travel beyond a small national production market.
   - The film was made by Kosmorama, not Nordisk Film; the case must not retroactively assign it to Nordisk merely because Nielsen is central to Danish film history.

2. **Atlantis (1913)**
   - Nordisk Film industrial/export case.
   - Production Case function: Nordisk's largest and most ambitious prewar production, literary prestige, transatlantic narrative scale, 35 mm feature production and market-specific endings.
   - The Russian ending is evidence of international versioning, not a license to invent arbitrary alternate cuts.

### P2 – book-only for Chapter 5

1. **L'Assassinat du duc de Guise (1908)**
   - Important Film d'Art/prestige evidence, but Queen Elizabeth and Fantômas carry more distinct Production Case functions.

2. **The White Slave Trade (1910)** / *Den hvide Slavehandel*
   - Important for Danish multi-reel competition, imitation and export distribution.
   - Keep book-only here because two same-title Danish productions and fragmentary survival require careful version history, while Afgrunden and Atlantis already cover the required Danish gameplay axes.

3. **L'Inferno (1911)**
   - Important Italian feature-length literary spectacle before Cabiria.
   - Book-only because Cabiria provides the stronger nonredundant production-scale case.

4. **Quo Vadis? (1913)**
   - Chapter 4 explicitly deferred this title to the Chapter 5 audit.
   - Chapter 5 resolves that deferral as P2: it is essential evidence for the Italian epic/export cycle, but a second epic Production Case would substantially duplicate Cabiria's production-learning function.

5. **The Student of Prague (1913)** / *Der Student von Prag*
   - Valuable German prewar art-film/star evidence and a bridge toward later German cinema.
   - Keep book-only rather than pre-empting the dedicated German silent-cinema chapter.

6. **Ingeborg Holm (1913)**
   - Valuable Swedish social-drama evidence.
   - Keep book-only here; Scandinavian silent cinema receives deeper treatment later.

7. **Raja Harishchandra (1913)**
   - Required book evidence that prewar feature production was not confined to Europe and the United States.
   - Do not tokenize Indian cinema with a shallow Production Case in this chapter; its production and exhibition history belongs to the later chapter on silent cinemas beyond the usual canon.

## Historical objects and systems – NO_PRODUCTION_CASE

These are chapter evidence but are **not** film productions and must not become fake Production Cases:

- **Pathé Frères production-distribution network** – vertically coordinated international production, catalogues, distribution and market power.
- **Gaumont production and serial infrastructure** – company organization enabling recurring characters and regular genre production.
- **Nordisk Film export network** – branch offices and foreign distribution that made a small national industry unusually international.
- **Italian historical-epic production centres** – Turin/Rome capital, sets, crowds and prestige infrastructure.
- **Transnational star circulation** – actors becoming marketable identities across borders.
- **Territorial rights, translated intertitles and versioning** – the legal/editorial work required to make films travel.
- **Film catalogues, trade press and export advertising** – industrial information systems through which international markets were built.

## Historiographic safeguards

1. **No national-invention ladder.** The chapter compares connected production centres; it does not say one country 'invented' international cinema.
2. **Company and film are not interchangeable.** Afgrunden must remain a Kosmorama production; Nordisk's international strategy is represented separately by Atlantis and company history.
3. **Star systems are contractual and distributive as well as aesthetic.** Bernhardt, Nielsen, Psilander and Navarre matter because their images circulated through production and market structures, not because fame alone equals a system.
4. **Feature length is plural.** Three-reel, four-reel, multi-part and very long productions coexisted; the chapter does not project a later standardized feature runtime backward.
5. **Version history is evidence.** Atlantis' alternative ending and Cabiria's 1914/1931 histories must stay explicitly versioned.
6. **Epic scale does not require duplicate cases.** Quo Vadis? remains important in the book even though Cabiria is the canonical Italian epic Production Case.
7. **P2 does not mean unimportant.** P2 means the film carries chapter evidence without enough distinct gameplay value to justify another full Production Case now.
8. **Global breadth must not become tokenism.** Raja Harishchandra is retained in the historical argument and deferred to a chapter able to treat Indian production/exhibition with adequate depth.
9. **Silent capture remains silent.** Later scores, live accompaniment and restoration presentations must not be described as synchronized original production sound.
