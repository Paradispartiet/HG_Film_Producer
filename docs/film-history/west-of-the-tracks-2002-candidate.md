# West of the Tracks (2002–2003) — candidate study

Chapter 27 · P2 · Not a canonical production case.

Wang Bing's documentary observes the transformation of Tiexi, an industrial district of Shenyang, through the experiences of workers and their communities. His 2002 interview situates the project at the turn of the millennium. BFI's analysis connects its sustained attention to the changing industrial landscape. A study must distinguish historical research from the film's particular viewpoint, rather than making individuals stand for an entire country's experience. ([interview](https://www.chinaindiefilm.org/interview-with-wang-bing-the-sense-of-history-and-destiny-2002/), [bfi](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/features/this-was-china-wang-bings-west-tracks))

Portable digital acquisition helped Wang sustain the project over a long period. Frieze identifies a borrowed Panasonic mini-DV camera and approximately 300 hours of footage, consistent in scale with the contemporary interview. This is a documented relationship between mobility and production duration, not an equipment specification sheet. A present-day exercise can use a modest camera, short authorized sessions and voluntary participants. ([frieze](https://www.frieze.com/ko/article/time-does-not-heal-inside-wang-bings-cinema-slowness), [interview](https://www.chinaindiefilm.org/interview-with-wang-bing-the-sense-of-history-and-destiny-2002/))

The completed documentary organizes its material into Rust, Remnants and Rails, tracing connections across work, housing and transport. The extended form required substantial editorial selection. Elena Pollacchi's academic chapter describes a two-year editing process and more than 300 hours of source material. Logging and preserving original media are appropriate teaching adaptations, not reconstructed claims about Wang's actual editing or storage hardware. ([bfi](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/features/this-was-china-wang-bings-west-tracks), [pollacchi](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/wang-bings-filmmaking-of-the-china-dream/history-in-the-making-the-debut-epic-tiexi-qu-west-of-the-tracks/22AD8B3DC4ACF6B5AC7A9E06B63C0A6C))

## Six-phase practice

- **Industrial history and access:** Prepare a research map for a fictional workplace transition, including permissions and independent historical sources.
- **Portable digital capture:** Plan three repeat visits to a permitted ordinary location with a small camera, consent and short recording sessions.
- **Logging and media preservation:** Design a simple shot log with consent status, date, location, topic and two backup copies for a small fictional archive.
- **Triptych and documentary montage:** Outline three linked chapters about an invented workplace, community and transport system.
- **Observation and sound context:** Record original ambience in a permitted ordinary space and label its relation to each fictional shot.
- **Finishing funds and version history:** Create a version ledger distinguishing the Berlin cut, Rotterdam triptych, finishing support and later screening records.

2002 Berlin and 2003 Rotterdam versions retain distinct provenance. No unsafe access or approved representation gap is claimed.
