# Searching (2018) — candidate study

Chapter 30 · P2 · Not a canonical production case.

Searching turns a missing-person thriller into a narrative presented through digital interfaces. Chaganty and co-writer/producer Sev Ohanian describe how an initial short-film idea became a feature at Bazelevs. The distinctive production question is how to coordinate live performances, graphic interfaces and audience attention rather than simply record a working computer screen. For learning purposes, interfaces and personal details should be fictional; no real account access or private information is needed. ([acv](https://www.asiancinevision.org/interview-aneesh-chaganty-sev-ohanian-searching/), [techcrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/08/25/searching-interview/))

The filmmakers developed a feature-length temporary version before principal photography. In their Asian CineVision interview they describe seven weeks of preparation with temporary images and Chaganty acting the roles himself. The reference film let the crew anticipate framing and narrative timing, and helped actors align their reactions with later screen graphics. The preparation was especially important because the live-action shoot lasted only thirteen days. ([acv](https://www.asiancinevision.org/interview-aneesh-chaganty-sev-ohanian-searching/), [techcrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/08/25/searching-interview/), [nfs](https://nofilmschool.com/2018/07/searching-sundance-aneesh-chaganty-interview))

Post-production treated the interface as a designed image. An Adobe interview describes Illustrator artwork animated in After Effects, connected to Premiere Pro editing. Frame.io's editors' account explains the unconventional editorial sequence, while cinematographer Juan Sebastian Baron describes how camera and editorial departments shared responsibility for directing attention inside the desktop. Reconstructing graphic elements and framing captured performances were connected but distinct tasks. ([adobe](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2018/02/06/director-aneesh-chaganty-brings-small-screen-big-screen-search), [frame](https://blog.frame.io/2018/09/10/made-in-frame-searching/), [baron](https://filmthreat.com/interviews/juan-sebastian-baron-cinematographer-for-searching/))

The finished image required selective color treatment: Adobe reports that the team graded the live-action material separately from established interface colors. USC's screening discussion also describes the extra work of adjusting on-screen language for foreign releases. These examples expose post-production and localization as central production costs. Reported editing durations differ across interviews; a short shooting schedule must not be mistaken for a short total production schedule. ([adobe](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2018/02/06/director-aneesh-chaganty-brings-small-screen-big-screen-search), [usc](https://cinema.usc.edu/news/article.cfm?id=35848), [nfs](https://nofilmschool.com/2018/07/searching-sundance-aneesh-chaganty-interview))

## Six-phase practice

- **Previsualization and pitch:** Create a one-minute animatic with placeholder footage and an invented screen interface.
- **Performance and capture:** Plan an actor's eyeline map with three timed cues on a fictional interface.
- **Interface and motion design:** Design two fictional vector windows and animate one meaningful transition.
- **Editing and visual attention:** Compare a wide desktop view with a tighter view of a fictional notification; justify the transition.
- **Color and compositing:** Plan a separate color correction for live footage and unchanged interface graphics.
- **Post schedule and localization:** Draft a delivery plan for two languages using fictional messages, with separate time for interface rebuilding and review.
