## Chapter 27 — digital production

1. **Atanarjuat: The Fast Runner (2001).** The Canadian Film Encyclopedia (TIFF) independently records that the feature is in Inuktitut and is a landmark in Inuit-directed, scripted and acted feature filmmaking. The earlier source was a generic BFI Arctic film list and did not directly establish this authorship. The article now cites the [TIFF entry](https://cfe.tiff.net/content/films/atanarjuat) alongside BFI's digital-film sources.
2. **West of the Tracks (2002; international screening 2003).** [BFI's catalogue](https://www.bfi.org.uk/film/32dceea7-1d44-5ca6-ac0c-c596fd635b0b/west-of-the-tracks) gives **2002** as the film's year. The [BFI critical essay](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/features/this-was-china-wang-bings-west-tracks) speaks about the film's 2003 entry into international circulation. [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/wang-bings-filmmaking-of-the-china-dream/history-in-the-making-the-debut-epic-tiexi-qu-west-of-the-tracks/22AD8B3DC4ACF6B5AC7A9E06B63C0A6C) documents the years of shooting and a full-length Rotterdam screening in **January 2003**. The essay now distinguishes photography, film year, and festival exhibition instead of pretending that a single date describes each event.

## Chapter 28 — transnational East Asian film histories

3. **Xiao Wu (1997/1998).** [BFI's film catalogue](https://www.bfi.org.uk/film/6bca4df4-7b4c-5f31-9b19-ebeb2438cec9/xiao-wu) calls Jia Zhangke's debut a **1997** film. The [Berlinale's retrospective programme](https://www.berlinale.de/en/2020/programme/202012567.html) records the film's **1998** appearance in the Forum section. The revision distinguishes the film year from its later international festival circulation.
4. **Chungking Express (1994).** Wong Kar-wai's [director's note for the Criterion restorations](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/7325-%20monde-de-wong-kar-wai-note-du-directeur) says that the film was initially shot and theatrically released at **1.66:1**, while certain subsequent video releases used **1.85:1**. The restored version returned to the earlier framing. This has been made explicit in the film's article.
5. **In the Mood for Love (2000).** The same director's note documents **1.66:1** original theatrical framing, **1.85:1** video versions, and subsequent work on the film's sound. The article now identifies specific version changes instead of treating a generic 'restoration' as an automatic return to one timeless original.

**Files:** `filmHistoryChapterTwentyEight.ts`, `filmHistoryChapterTwentyEightChinaKorea.ts`, `filmHistoryChapterTwentyEightHongKongTaiwan.ts`, `filmHistoryChapterTwentyEight.test.ts`.
