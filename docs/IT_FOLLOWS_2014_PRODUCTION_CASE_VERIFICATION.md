## Production system

Film-specific system:

`detroit_timeless_suburb_walking_curse_spatial_dread`

The system joins:

- David Robert Mitchell's recurring childhood nightmare of a slow, relentless pursuer;
- a simple transmitted curse whose changing human form always walks toward its current target;
- independent financing secured before casting;
- Detroit-area streets, houses, schools, ruins, lakeside spaces and a pool climax;
- Michael T. Perry's intentionally unstable mixture of 1950s through contemporary objects and invented technology;
- Maika Monroe's vulnerable physical performance and a believable young friend-group ensemble;
- screenplay-defined entity forms and extensive form-by-form casting;
- months of shot-by-shot planning and storyboarding by Mitchell and Mike Gioulakis;
- mostly ARRI ALEXA capture with RED Epic for specialist wheelchair-rig and underwater work;
- Cooke S4 lenses, frequent 18 mm deep-focus coverage and 2.35:1 spatial scanning;
- realistic threat distance rather than enlarged monster inserts;
- objective, almost robotic pans and zooms contrasted with limited subjective handheld work;
- Julio C. Perez IV's exact, minimal editing and extended waiting intervals;
- detailed temp music and sound before an accelerated Cannes finish;
- Disasterpeace's approximately three-week synthesis score and credited sound-design contribution;
- a balance between melodic beauty, environmental quiet and abrasive controlled noise.

The film is not treated as generic supernatural horror or as a sex-equals-death allegory. Its production identity depends on how threat rules, timeless design, wide digital space, patient recognition, performance, editing and electronic sound force the audience to keep searching ordinary surroundings.
