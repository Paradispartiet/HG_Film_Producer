## Production system

The case is not modelled as a generic family-animation or feelings story. It is treated as one coordinated production system:

- Pete Docter's observation of his daughter's changing behaviour and consultation with Paul Ekman and Dacher Keltner;
- a screenplay that compresses a larger emotion field into Joy, Sadness, Fear, Anger and Disgust and makes Sadness structurally necessary;
- parallel outer-family and inner-mind action rather than an explanatory frame around Riley;
- Ralph Eggleston's split between a tactile real world and a saturated, functional mental geography;
- Patrick Lin's virtual camera-and-staging grammar and Kim White's lighting grammar;
- energy-particle emotion bodies, iridescent surfaces, volumetric glow and the first production use of geometry light for Joy;
- Kevin Nolting's iterative story-reel editing and crosscutting between Riley and Headquarters;
- Ren Klyce and Shannon Mills's designed sound distinctions between physical and mental spaces;
- Michael Giacchino's atmospheric, inward score;
- Cannes presentation and Academy recognition for both animation and screenplay.

## Coverage decisions

The two mapped fields are deliberate rather than gaps:

- `costume_makeup`: colour, silhouette, hair, surfaces and clothing perform identity work, but conventional costume and makeup are not the central character pipeline.
- `camera_format`: the image is governed by virtual lenses, framing, staging and movement, not by a physical acquisition format.

`documentary_method` is `not_central`: family observation and psychology consultation inform the fiction, but the film remains a fully scripted and fabricated animated feature.
