## Production system

Noi the Albino is treated as an **Icelandic fjord-youth entrapment system**, not as a generic quirky Nordic comedy or a picturesque coming-of-age film.

The playable model connects:

- the early-2000s New Icelandic Wave and Dagur Kári's Danish Film School network;
- a character and idea collection developed over roughly twelve years;
- Icelandic, German, British and Danish production and sales partners;
- a snow-dependent shoot in the Westfjords and a settlement designed as a plausible invented universe;
- Tómas Lemarquis as the professional centre of a largely locally recruited ensemble;
- school, family home, petrol station, bank, graveyard and basement as a closed social geography;
- Rasmus Videbæk's controlled 35 mm contrast between dangerous white exteriors and dark green-grey interiors;
- Jón Steinar Ragnarsson's everyday village design and tropical escape images;
- Linda B. Arnardóttir, Tanja Dehmel and Frida Metusalemsdottir's restrained winter appearance system;
- Daniel Dencik's deadpan routines, delayed reactions and sudden catastrophic rupture;
- Pétur Einarsson's sparse dialogue, wind, vehicles, interiors and avalanche silence;
- Dagur Kári and Orri Jónsson's Slowblow score merging melancholy, humour and fragile hope;
- the avalanche converting Nói's fantasy of escape into a material reordering of his world.
