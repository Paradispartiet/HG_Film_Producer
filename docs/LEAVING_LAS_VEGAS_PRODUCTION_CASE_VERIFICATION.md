## Production system

Producer Stuart Regen optioned John O'Brien's novel and passed it to Mike Figgis after the director's conflicts with larger studio productions. Figgis adapted the dark, dialogue-heavy book without imposing recovery, cure or a conventional happy ending. The low budget then protected that choice by reducing the number of external decision-makers able to reshape the screenplay.

The coordinated system combines:

- John O'Brien's novel and Figgis's cut-and-reassemble adaptation process;
- the relationship rule that Sera will not ask Ben to stop drinking;
- a roughly four-million-dollar budget and twenty-eight-day schedule;
- reduced fees from Figgis and Nicolas Cage to protect the production;
- a French-British-American production mounted through Initial Productions;
- MGM/UA acquisition and distribution after earlier specialty-company rejection;
- two weeks of Cage-Figgis rehearsal at the Chateau Marmont;
- Cage's physiological research into alcoholism and bodily performance;
- Elisabeth Shue's research with Las Vegas sex workers and first-day therapist improvisations;
- Vivienne Westwood's donated costumes for Sera;
- small Aaton-scale Super 16 equipment instead of a conventional 35mm unit;
- Declan Quinn lighting and photographing the master while Figgis operated second camera;
- simultaneous intimate coverage, minimal lighting, handheld or tripod support and no crane system;
- Las Vegas, Los Angeles and Laughlin locations shaped by casino restrictions and a small mobile crew;
- vehicle-based and uncontrolled street filming that preserves actual city movement;
- John Smith's credited edit and the delayed, optically constructed main titles;
- Mike Figgis's own blues-jazz score, instrumental performance and track production;
- Sting standards and a musical progression described as moving toward operatic scale;
- later restoration and continuing repertory circulation.
