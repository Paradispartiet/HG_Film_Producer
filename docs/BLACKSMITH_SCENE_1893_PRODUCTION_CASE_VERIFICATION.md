# Blacksmith Scene (1893) — Production Case verification

Verified: **2026-08-14**  
Scenario: `scenario_blacksmith_scene_1893`

## Why this case belongs in Film Atlas

*Blacksmith Scene* is not being added merely because it is famous or early. It supplies a production architecture that the Atlas did not previously contain: a staged sub-minute performance created inside the Edison laboratory system, photographed with the Kinetograph for Kinetoscope viewing and dependent on the purpose-built Black Maria's ability to control strong natural light.

The case therefore teaches **apparatus + studio architecture + performers + light + fixed framing + exhibition format as one production system**. It also prevents two common historical shortcuts: calling the scene an untouched documentary record, and treating Thomas Edison as the film's director.

## Source-backed production claims

### Staged performance

MoMA describes three apparent blacksmiths played by Edison laboratory staff. Library of Congress identifies Charles Kayser, John Ott and a third unidentified employee. The gameplay therefore treats the blacksmith action as an arranged performance rather than an unmediated workplace observation.

### Makers and company context

MoMA attributes the making of the film to **William Heise and William K. L. Dickson** and explicitly distinguishes their filmmaking work from Edison's company oversight. The Production Case metadata follows that distinction instead of crediting Edison as director.

### Camera and editing

MoMA documents a **fixed camera** and a **single unedited take** lasting roughly half a minute. The editing lesson is consequently the absence of later continuity construction: action, timing and ensemble spacing must carry the complete event inside one shot.

### Black Maria and light

MoMA and the National Park Service document the Black Maria as a purpose-built motion-picture studio. NPS records the building's pivot around a circular track and its opening roof, allowing the studio to follow and admit sunlight. Lighting is therefore a source-verified production-design constraint rather than a generic aesthetic choice.

### Kinetograph and Kinetoscope

MoMA identifies Kinetograph capture for Kinetoscope viewing. The case keeps camera and exhibition apparatus linked and does not retroactively treat an 1893 production as if it had been organized around later projection-era assumptions.

### Exhibition and preservation

Library of Congress records the May 9, 1893 Brooklyn Institute of Arts and Sciences Kinetoscope demonstration. The complete National Film Registry listing records *Blacksmith Scene* as an 1893 title selected to the Registry in 1995.
