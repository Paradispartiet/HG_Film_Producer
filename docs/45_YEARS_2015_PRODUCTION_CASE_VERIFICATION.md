## Production system

*45 Years* is modeled as a British 35 mm five-day marriage-memory chamber drama built from:

- Andrew Haigh's expansion of David Constantine's approximately ten-page story;
- an added wedding-anniversary countdown that gives the feature five escalating days;
- a deliberate point-of-view shift from Geoff to Kate;
- The Bureau production with BFI Film Fund and Film4 backing;
- Charlotte Rampling and Tom Courtenay's unrehearsed, behaviour-led two-person performance;
- Norfolk domestic and rural locations;
- Lol Crawley's low-budget 35 mm Panavision image system;
- exact blocking, long two-shots and compositions that increasingly privilege Kate;
- the cottage, attic, slides, projector and anniversary hall as a material memory archive;
- Jonathan Alberts's five-part daily structure and editorial repetition;
- Joakim Sundström's slide-carousel, creak, wind and room-tone motifs;
- source songs written into the screenplay, culminating in the extended final dance take.

The film is therefore not assigned through British nationality, old age, romance or domestic setting alone. Its production logic joins adaptation, performance, shared-frame blocking, analogue memory objects, repeated sound and source music into one relationship system.

### Scenes from a Marriage

The closest comparison supplies an actor-led marital chamber system built from shared rooms, speech, silence, close observation and changing emotional power. *45 Years* compresses that relationship scrutiny into five days and shifts the destabilization through Kate's largely unspoken discoveries.

### Still Walking

The second comparison supplies domestic routine, household geography, an absent person and layered everyday sound as carriers of memory. *45 Years* turns the attic and slide projector into a more concentrated archive whose objects rewrite the marriage.

### The Son's Room

The third comparison supplies a family structure reorganized by a dead or absent person, repeated routines and measured emotional time. *45 Years* makes the absent figure predate the marriage and lets her return through evidence rather than a newly occurring loss.
