## Production system

The case models the film as a fact-based regional journey rather than as a generic gentle road movie:

- Mary Sweeney and John Roach develop Alvin Straight's real 1994 lawn-mower trip into a linear screenplay built from practical setbacks, roadside encounters, memory and reconciliation.
- Sweeney's project moves rapidly from Lynch reading the script in June 1998 to StudioCanal financing and a late-summer/early-autumn production.
- Picture Factory follows the real route through Iowa and Wisconsin rather than substituting a generic landscape.
- Principal photography proceeds chronologically during harvest, allowing weather, scenery, travel duration, beard growth and physical fatigue to accumulate inside the fiction.
- Richard Farnsworth's own damaged hip, canes and identification with Alvin's independence inform a restrained bodily performance.
- Jack Fisk and Patricia Norris organize towns, farms, roadsides, camps, homes, workwear, the mower and its trailer as one practical Midwestern material world.
- Freddie Francis's final feature credit uses 35 mm anamorphic Panavision photography, open fields, aerial scale, gliding movement and dissolves to make extremely slow travel visually expansive.
- Mary Sweeney's editing protects duration and episodic encounter; sound and music sometimes motivate recutting so images give them room.
- David Lynch and the credited sound team make mower engine, traffic, wind, insects, storms, machinery, voices and silence carry movement and danger.
- Angelo Badalamenti's restrained score works as a precise layer within the road sound rather than as continuous emotional instruction.
- Cannes competition and Richard Farnsworth's Academy Award nomination establish the film's immediate institutional recognition.
