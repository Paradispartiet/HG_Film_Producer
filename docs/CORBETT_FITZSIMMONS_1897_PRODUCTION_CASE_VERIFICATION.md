- Priority: **P0 Anchor Film**
- Canonical filmmaker credit: **Enoch J. Rector**
- Production context: **Dan Stuart / Veriscope Company**
- Runtime model: approximately **100 minutes** for the long-form event case
- Production type: nonfiction sporting-event / exhibition-system case

## Why this is a distinct Production Case

The learning value is not simply that a boxing match was photographed. The case joins the entire chain:

1. a real championship event whose duration had to be covered rather than reduced to a brief novelty;
2. several adjacent cameras and planned reel changes to preserve the unfolding event;
3. a proprietary wide film format;
4. a matching Veriscope projection requirement;
5. live expert commentary as part of theatrical presentation rather than synchronized sound;
6. territorial and local exhibition arrangements;
7. event-style ticket economics and audience demand.

This makes production, distribution and exhibition inseparable in a way that is not duplicated by the Chapter 1 cases.

## Source pack

### Library of Congress

`https://www.loc.gov/item/2023602024/`

Controls the canonical attribution and version history. It identifies Rector with production/photography, Dan Stuart with the Veriscope enterprise, the fourteen-round event, special projection requirements, live commentary, territorial distribution and the New York premiere. It also explicitly separates Sigmund Lubin's later facsimile production and warns against treating William A. Brady as producer of the Veriscope original.

### National Science and Media Museum

`https://blog.scienceandmediamuseum.org.uk/corbett-fitzsimmons-fight-film-fragment/`

Supports the three-camera system, very large quantity of exposed film, long-form event coverage, premiere history and the fact that only fragments of the original survive.

### University of California Press / JSTOR

`https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.1525/j.ctt1pnd60.9`

Dan Streible's *Fight Pictures* provides the scholarly historiographic frame for understanding boxing films as a system of event recording, promotion, exhibition and spectatorship rather than a purely technical novelty.

### American Film Institute

`https://catalog.afi.com/Film/28825-CORBETT-AND-FITZSIMMONS-FIGHT`

Used narrowly for duration and three-camera evidence. AFI's catalogue metadata folds later Lubin/Brady material into the entry, so it **does not control original-production attribution**. Where the records conflict, the more explicit Library of Congress separation of the Veriscope original and Lubin reproduction governs the canonical case.

## Permanent historiographic safeguards

- Do not credit William A. Brady as producer of the original merely because later catalogue records conflate histories; he is kept in Corbett's managerial/promotional context.
- Do not collapse Sigmund Lubin's later staged/condensed facsimile into Rector's Veriscope original.
- Do not treat live expert commentary as synchronized production sound.
- Do not treat the proprietary wide format as only an image-format fact; the matching projector materially shaped exhibition control.
- Do not fabricate a complete modern master from the fragmentary surviving original.
- Do not turn nonfiction event structure into fictional screenplay beats.
