## Production question

How can a very short view of everyday labor be actively produced through apparatus, location choice, fixed framing, crowd movement, start timing and repeat filming without either importing later continuity grammar or pretending that actuality is an untouched transparent record?

## Source pack

1. **Institut Lumière — Le Cinématographe Lumière**  
   Supports the Cinématographe system, the 22 March 1895 restricted demonstration using *Sortie d'usine*, subsequent projection history and the 28 December commercial programme.
2. **British Film Institute — In the beginning: cinema's murky origin story**  
   Places the Lumière apparatus among competing early moving-image systems and documents the compact, reliable Cinématographe and short-roll production constraint.
3. **Library of Congress — Documentary and Actuality Films**  
   Places *Workers Leaving the Lumière Factory* inside the early actuality tradition and supports the distinction between nonfiction subject matter and a claim of automatic neutrality.
4. **Australian Centre for the Moving Image — Workers Leaving the Lumiere Factory**  
   Catalogues distinct surviving versions and provides an institutional cross-check for treating version history as part of the Production Case.

## Historiographic safeguards

- Do **not** present this film as proof of a single inventor or a single uncontested birthday for cinema.
- Do **not** equate actuality with untouched or transparent reality; apparatus placement, framing and timing are production decisions.
- Do **not** collapse the surviving filmed versions into one imaginary uncontested original. Each version is treated as a distinct production iteration.
- Do **not** retrofit later continuity editing onto the single-view construction.
- Do **not** attribute later musical accompaniment or restoration sound to the original 1895 capture.
