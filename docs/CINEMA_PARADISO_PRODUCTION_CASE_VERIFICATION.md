## Production system

The case treats *Cinema Paradiso* as a cinema-memory production system built from one communal movie house, projection labour, censorship, changing film exhibition and an adult return structure rather than as generic nostalgia.

Its production logic joins:

- an Italian-French late-1980s production looking back at postwar Sicily
- three performers carrying Salvatore through childhood, adolescence and adulthood
- the projection booth as workplace, classroom, danger zone and archive
- the parish cinema, village square and changing auditorium as communal history
- clerical censorship and the physical handling of film fragments
- Blasco Giurato's integration of projected images, booth machinery and village life
- Andrea Crisanti's cinema, square and period environment
- Mario Morra's memory structure and the materially different theatrical and Director's Cuts
- projection noise, audience reaction, bells, fire and changing room acoustics
- Ennio and Andrea Morricone's recurring themes as emotional memory
- the final recovered montage of censored kisses
- Cannes, Oscar and BAFTA recognition followed by repeated restoration and re-release
