## Production thesis

Café Society is treated as a coordinated 1930s Hollywood–New York period-romance system rather than as generic Woody Allen dialogue comedy. The production joins Bobby Dorfman's divided romantic and social trajectory to a deliberately split visual world: luminous Hollywood promise and darker New York nightclub society.

Vittorio Storaro's first long-form digital feature uses Sony F65/F55 capture, Cooke optics, a 2:1 Univisium frame, controlled lighting and a linked 4K color pipeline to make place and emotional phase visible. Santo Loquasto combines surviving Los Angeles architecture with recreated New York interiors and clubs. Suzy Benzinger's wardrobe establishes 1930s social position and glamour, while period jazz — especially Rodgers and Hart material and Vince Giordano and the Nighthawks — turns the nightclub setting into an audible historical environment.

## Donor logic

### Midnight in Paris

The nearest donor for Allen's period-city construction. It provides a verified model for romantic nostalgia, practical location transformation, historical dressing, color and optics used to distinguish imagined pasts.

### Her

The urban-romance donor. It provides a verified comparison for city architecture, restrained performance, emotional color and a romantic structure in which environment and image carry interior states.

### Moonrise Kingdom

The controlled-period-design donor. It provides a verified comparison for researched costume, deliberately authored period geography, color, music and precise visual construction without confusing design with mere decoration.

`Manhattan` and `Blue Jasmine` were considered during research but are not used as donors because they are not materialized Film Study profiles in the current Constructed Worlds catalog.
