# Traffic in Souls (1913) – Production Case verification

## Canonical identity

- Scenario ID: `scenario_traffic_in_souls_1913`
- Title: *Traffic in Souls*
- Alternate AFI title: *While New York Sleeps: A Photodrama of Today*
- Year: 1913
- Production company: Independent Moving Picture Co. (IMP)
- Distribution company: Universal Film Manufacturing Co.
- Director: George Loane Tucker
- Scenario writers: Walter MacNamara and George Loane Tucker
- Scenario runtime model: 88 minutes, following UCLA's Library of Congress preservation-print listing
- Release-scale boundary: AFI records six to seven reels; UCLA describes a six-reel completed cut
- Chapter role: final Film History Chapter 4 P1 case for American feature economics, independent production, Universal distribution and contemporary social-problem melodrama
- Verification date: 2026-08-14

## What is verified

1. **IMP production and Universal distribution.** AFI records these as separate industrial functions.
2. **Feature-scale silent release.** AFI lists six to seven reels, black-and-white and silent; UCLA lists an 88-minute 35 mm preservation print.
3. **Direction and writing.** AFI and UCLA identify George Loane Tucker as director; AFI credits Tucker and Walter MacNamara with the scenario.
4. **Secret production and collaborative completion.** UCLA reports that the film was cast and shot without IMP officials' knowledge while management remained sceptical of features, and that Jack Cohn and Walter MacNamara cut the material to six reels after Tucker left for England.
5. **Real New York locations.** UCLA identifies actual New York location filming as part of the surviving film's historical value.
6. **Commercial feature experiment.** UCLA reports a $5,700 investment, $450,000 return and simultaneous exhibition at 20 New York theatres. BFI places the film among early American features and stresses its non-literary, contemporary, street-level strategy.
7. **Copyright-deposit identity.** The Library of Congress records Universal Film Manufacturing Company as copyright claimant under registration LU 1767.
8. **National preservation significance.** The Library of Congress added the film to the National Film Registry in 2006.
9. **Publicity and approval are not equivalent.** AFI records that advertising invoked Rockefeller investigations, while John D. Rockefeller Jr. denied authorization, approval or sanction.
10. **The film is staged melodrama.** Its plot, reform rhetoric and period “white slavery” terminology must not be presented as neutral documentary evidence about prostitution, migration or organized crime.

## Historiographic safeguards

The case does **not** claim that:

- *Traffic in Souls* invented the American feature;
- IMP and Universal were the same production function in this release;
- Universal directed the film;
- George Loane Tucker alone completed every stage of post-production;
- the Rockefeller Commission, Bureau of Social Hygiene or John D. Rockefeller Jr. approved the film;
- the film's staged criminal network is documentary proof of one real organization;
- period “white slavery” language is a neutral scientific category;
- the 2012 accompaniment is a synchronized original 1913 score;
- an 88-minute preservation print proves that every 1913 release copy had identical length and content.

Instead, the film is taught as a commercially consequential American feature experiment whose industrial innovation, location work, parallel melodrama, reform publicity and sensational exploitation must be analysed together but not conflated.
