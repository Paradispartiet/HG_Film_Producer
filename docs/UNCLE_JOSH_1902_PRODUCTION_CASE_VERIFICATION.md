## Why this is a distinct Production Case

The case is not included to prove that historical audiences were unable to understand projected images. Its production value lies in making the **projection situation itself** playable:

1. a theatre box/stage and screen must remain legible in one compact setup;
2. three projected attractions — dancer, express train and country couple — create a controlled comic escalation;
3. Charles Manley's physical reactions connect the fictional spectator to each projected image;
4. the final destruction of the screen exposes the operator and turns hidden exhibition machinery into the payoff;
5. the player must keep the comic rube stereotype separate from claims about actual historical audience behaviour.

## Permanent safeguards

- **No naïve-audience shortcut.** Uncle Josh is a scripted comic spectator, not documentary proof of universal audience panic.
- **No synchronized-sound fiction.** The photographed film is silent; train noise, dialogue or music are not canonical synchronized production sound.
- **No invisible-apparatus simplification.** Screen, theatre space and operator reveal are part of the production system and must remain spatially readable.
- **No modern coverage retrofit.** The case preserves the compact film-within-film staging instead of rebuilding it as later shot/reverse-shot grammar.
- **No auteur inflation.** Porter is the canonical maker supported by LOC/AFI, without inventing a modern departmental credit structure not established by the surviving record.
