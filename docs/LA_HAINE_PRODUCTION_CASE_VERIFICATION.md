## Production system

The project began after Mathieu Kassovitz encountered the mourning around the April 1993 police killing of seventeen-year-old Makomé M'Bowolé. He postponed another project, called his producer that night, wrote La Haine in three to four months and constructed the screenplay backward from its fatal ending.

The coordinated system combines:

- a twenty-four-hour structure built from a known ending;
- three symbolic but behaviorally distinct friends whose disagreements keep scenes in motion;
- French social realism, banlieue cinema, hip-hop politics and influences from Eisenstein, Scorsese and Spike Lee;
- production by Les Productions Lazennec with Canal+, La Sept Cinéma and Kasso Inc.;
- preparation and an eight-week shoot in Chanteloup-les-Vignes;
- residents recruited as extras and a production relationship built inside the housing project;
- wide compositions, crane and Steadicam movement in the banlieue;
- a reduced Paris unit using one camera, long lenses and guerrilla-style shooting;
- Pierre Aïm's black-and-white 35mm photography and 1.85:1 frame;
- a deliberate shift from environmental width to compressed city proximity;
- Mathieu Kassovitz and Scott Stevenson's clock-driven, episodic edit;
- Vincent Tulli's offscreen sound world, inspired by American Graffiti;
- ticking, gunshots, level changes and ambiguous unseen action;
- no conventional score-led emotional system, with songs emerging from radios, cars and local space;
- continuing preservation through Cannes, César, BFI, Criterion and supervised 4K restoration.
