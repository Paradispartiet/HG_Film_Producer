## Production system

Vincent Gallo and Alison Bagnall's screenplay turns Billy Brown's release from prison into a regional homecoming built around a fake marriage, a hostile family visit and a revenge fantasy tied to Buffalo football history. Gallo's contemporaneous account traces the script to 1989 and a later rewrite after Buffalo's Super Bowl loss.

The production system joins:

- a compact Chris Hanley, Cinepix and Lions Gate independent production
- a twenty-three-day shoot in and around Buffalo
- real local architecture, winter streets, diners, bowling spaces and the filmmaker's remembered family geography
- tightly scripted rather than improvised performances
- Lance Acord's feature-debut cinematography
- scarce 35 mm reversal stock requiring precise exposure, heavy light and preplanned colour
- Gideon Ponte and James Chinlund's controlled regional interiors and surfaces
- Curtiss Clayton's abrupt memory, fantasy and subjective-time transitions
- Vincent Gallo's original score and songs by King Crimson, Yes, Stan Getz and others
- theatrical lighting transformations and a multi-camera frozen gunshot tableau

The result is not generic low-budget realism. Buffalo itself becomes an emotional production tool: a remembered city of cold surfaces, football mythology, family shame, arrested style and imagined escape.
