## Production system

The case models the film as one integrated labour-and-body system rather than as generic poverty drama:

- Jean-Pierre and Luc Dardenne transform their documentary background into rigorously staged fiction.
- Films du Fleuve, Association des Réalisateurs Producteurs, RTBF, Canal+ and public-film support form the Belgian-French production base.
- Postindustrial Seraing and Liège geography turns roads, factories, employment offices, a caravan site, a pond and a waffle stand into material obstacles.
- The script is structured through dismissal, job seeking, concealment, betrayal, survival procedure and consequence rather than explanatory backstory.
- Émilie Dequenne was selected as an unknown performer so the audience encounters Rosetta rather than an established star persona.
- Dequenne's running, lifting, fighting, breathing and guarded speech make social exclusion a physical performance.
- Alain Marcoen's Super 16 image, 35 mm blow-up and 1.66:1 presentation preserve grain, mobility and bodily proximity.
- Camera operator Benoît Dervaux describes the handheld camera as an actor inside extensively rehearsed choreography.
- The camera accepts backs, walls, doors, incomplete views and physical collisions instead of stabilizing the world around Rosetta.
- Igor Gabriel's design and the practical object system make gas bottles, tools, food, fuel and work materially interchangeable.
- Marie-Hélène Dozo cuts rehearsed sequence material and multiple positions into forward pressure without explanatory release.
- Jean-Pierre Duret and the sound team use wind, mud, water, machinery, breath, doors, a hair dryer and Riquet's scooter as recurring signals.
- Jean-Pierre Cocco is credited for original music, but environmental and bodily sound remain structurally dominant.
- The unanimous Palme d'Or and Émilie Dequenne's Best Actress prize established the film's international legacy.
