## Production system

The case models the film as one integrated nocturnal production system rather than as a generic erotic mystery:

- Stanley Kubrick and Frederic Raphael relocate Arthur Schnitzler's `Traumnovelle` from Vienna to contemporary Manhattan.
- Pole Star, Hobby Films and Warner Bros. support an unusually prolonged 1996–1998 British production.
- Manhattan is reconstructed through four Pinewood street blocks, New York research material, English locations and estate architecture.
- Tom Cruise and Nicole Kidman's then-real marriage becomes part of the film's controlled performance tension.
- Kubrick's extended rehearsal-and-take method, performer replacements and repeated location changes construct heightened rather than ordinary realism.
- Larry Smith and Kubrick use Arriflex 535B cameras, Moviecam SL Steadicam, Zeiss Super Speed primes, frequent 18 mm framing and 1.85:1 composition.
- Christmas lights, converted street lamps, practical fixtures, China balls and architectural rigs support actor movement and 360-degree staging.
- Two-stop force development and near-wide-open exposure produce grain, saturated colour, warm practical glow and deep night blacks.
- Roy Walker and Leslie Tomkins's design joins affluent interiors, medical and domestic spaces, costume commerce, ritual architecture and an English reconstruction of New York.
- Venetian masks, cloaks and formal clothing turn class access, anonymity and sexual display into a visible hierarchy.
- Nigel Galt's extended edit coordinates recurrence, dream transitions, withheld explanation, ritual repetition and Bill's circular return.
- Jocelyn Pook's sparse piano and ritual music make desire and threat feel repetitive rather than conventionally suspenseful.
- The United States theatrical version used digitally inserted foreground figures to obscure parts of the ritual sequence.
