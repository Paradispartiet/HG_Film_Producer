## Production system

Dogville is not treated as generic minimalist theatre. Its system joins:

- Lars von Trier's prologue-and-nine-chapter moral parable;
- post-Dogme digital experimentation with Brechtian and *Our Town*-like theatrical suggestion;
- a Denmark-led six-country Zentropa production;
- Peter Grant's named chalk floor plan, sparse furniture and selected practical structures;
- Nicole Kidman and an international ensemble performing walls, doors, labour and violence through gesture and shared spatial memory;
- John Hurt's literary narration and announced chapter outcomes;
- Anthony Dod Mantle and von Trier's mobile digital camera operation;
- hundreds of stage lights, live dimmer control and a camera-mounted eye light;
- Sony mini-DV coverage, CineAlta overhead passes and composited bird's-eye views;
- Molly Malene Stensgaard's chapter-and-performance assembly;
- Per Streit and Kristian Eidnes Andersen's sound-created doors, weather, work and unseen environment;
- recurring Baroque music as formal counterpoint.

The result is a constructed town whose missing walls increase social exposure: every inhabitant can be seen, every private act belongs to the community's visible moral map, and sound supplies the material world that the set refuses to imitate.
