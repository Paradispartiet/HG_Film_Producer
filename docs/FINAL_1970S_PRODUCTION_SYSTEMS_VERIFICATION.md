# Final 1970s Production Systems verification

Audit date: **2026-07-24**

This verification closes the three remaining unverified Production Cases from the 1970s without treating them as one artificial stylistic batch. Each film is integrated into the existing Film Study family that best describes its actual production logic.

## A Clockwork Orange (1971)

**Family:** Constructed worlds and institutional control

The profile treats the film as a coordinated near-future system built from contemporary British modernist architecture, transformed practical locations, class-coded costume, changing photographic regimes, Nadsat narration, institutional procedure and electronic-classical musical counterpoint.

Primary source set:

- AFI Catalog
- American Cinematographer
- British Film Institute film record
- British Film Institute location study

## Amarcord (1973)

**Family:** European poetic memory

The profile treats the film as a reconstructed communal memory rather than a factual Rimini chronicle. Its production system joins a seasonal episodic screenplay, Cinecittà's imaginary town, grotesque ensemble performance, Danilo Donati's designed world, Giuseppe Rotunno's colour photography, artificial weather and sea, postsynchronised sound and Nino Rota's recurring memory theme.

Primary source set:

- The Criterion Collection film record
- The Criterion Collection historical essay
- British Film Institute film record
- Festival de Cannes archive

## Scenes from a Marriage (1974)

**Family:** Family performance, grief and verbal power

The profile treats the work as an actor-led public-service television production: a six-part script, deliberately low-risk 16 mm production, small mobile unit, cramped interiors, accelerated schedule, collaborative Liv Ullmann–Erland Josephson performances, Sven Nykvist close-ups, optical mono dialogue and later theatrical compression.

Primary source set:

- Ingmar Bergman Foundation production archive
- Ingmar Bergman Foundation media archive
- The Criterion Collection film record
- The Criterion Collection historical essay
