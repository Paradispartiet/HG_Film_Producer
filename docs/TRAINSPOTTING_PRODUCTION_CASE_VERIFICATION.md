## Production system

Danny Boyle, producer Andrew Macdonald and screenwriter John Hodge reunited after Shallow Grave to adapt Irvine Welsh's fragmented Scots-language novel as a low-budget, culturally specific British film. Hodge made Renton the organising voice, using voice-over and a forward journey to hold together episodes, interior monologues and rapidly changing tones.

The coordinated system combines:

- Irvine Welsh's episodic 1993 novel and Scottish countercultural language;
- John Hodge's decision to organise the adaptation around Renton;
- Boyle's refusal of immediate Hollywood offers after Shallow Grave;
- a roughly £1.5 million budget and quick local production;
- Channel Four Films, Figment Films and Noel Gay Motion Picture Company;
- PolyGram's original cinema and home-video distribution;
- Calton Athletic Recovery Group research and on-set advice;
- Ewan McGregor's physical preparation and ensemble regional speech;
- Glasgow locations standing in for most of Edinburgh;
- brief Edinburgh and London location material completing the geography;
- Brian Tufano's photochemical colour photography and 1.85 presentation;
- low, overhead, distorted and rapidly moving viewpoints;
- Kave Quinn's saturated, decayed and deliberately theatrical spaces;
- Rachael Fleming's youth-cultural costume system;
- practical subjective constructions for the toilet dive and sinking overdose floor;
- prosthetic injection and bodily deterioration details;
- Masahiro Hirakubo's credited kinetic montage and ellipsis;
- Renton's voice-over joining literary language to subjective image and sound;
- rock, techno, glam, ambient and Britpop tracks used as structural sequence engines;
- an aggressive publicity and soundtrack identity that crossed into mainstream culture;
- later uncut restoration supervised by Boyle.
