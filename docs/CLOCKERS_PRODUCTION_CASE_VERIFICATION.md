## Production system

The project began as a Universal adaptation of Richard Price's novel with Martin Scorsese and Robert De Niro attached. After Scorsese moved to Casino, Spike Lee took over, developed a new draft with Price and redirected the story toward Strike, Brooklyn and the social consequences of the crack economy.

The coordinated system combines:

- Richard Price's researched literary crime world and Lee's screenplay revision;
- Universal scale with 40 Acres and a Mule authorship;
- Gowanus Houses transformed into the fictional Nelson Mandela Projects;
- two weeks of NYPD crime-scene research and reconstructed victim photographs;
- established actors working with first-time feature performers Mekhi Phifer and Thomas Jefferson Byrd;
- Andrew McAlpine's use of Bernie Imes's Juke Joint and related Brooklyn, Mississippi, Senegalese and Caribbean color-pattern references;
- Malik Sayeed's first feature as director of photography;
- eight weeks of stock testing and specially ordered Kodak 5239 Ektachrome;
- cross-processing in a negative bath under close Technicolor supervision;
- roughly three quarters of the feature photographed on the reversal stock;
- scene-specific 35mm and 16mm stocks, 1.85:1 framing, swing-and-tilt lenses and selective anamorphic distortion;
- large HMIs, Kinoflos, reflective surfaces, naturalistic fill and demanding exposure pulls;
- subjective flashbacks, reconstructed stills, moving-light effects and a practical sled rig;
- Sam Pollard's credited edit, Skip Lievsay's sound-design department and Terence Blanchard's score;
- archive preservation of the screenplay, still photography and release materials.
