## Production system

Film-specific system:

`quebec_35mm_square_frame_mother_son_pop_music_release`

The system joins:

- Xavier Dolan's fictional near-future Québec law allowing a parent to transfer a dangerous child to institutional custody;
- a screenplay centred on the unstable household formed by widowed mother Die, her son Steve and neighbour Kyla;
- Anne Dorval, Antoine Olivier Pilon and Suzanne Clément's physically volatile but mutually dependent three-person performance;
- Metafilms production led by Nancy Grant and Xavier Dolan;
- Dolan and André Turpin's transfer of the square-frame experiment from the College Boy music video to a colour 35 mm feature;
- a predominantly 1:1 frame designed for portraiture, direct eye contact and vertical bodily concentration;
- two controlled expansions toward 1.85:1 at moments of imagined freedom rather than a permanently available widescreen world;
- Colombe Raby's suburban house, street, karaoke, study and institutional spaces;
- Dolan's character-defining costumes and Maïna Militza's makeup;
- editing by Dolan, conceived together with scene rhythm and music while writing;
- François Grenon, Sylvain Brassard and the sound team enlarging the restricted image through dialogue overlap, impacts, traffic, karaoke and quiet;
- a character-owned mix-CD logic in which familiar pop songs become shared memory, performance and emotional release;
- Cannes, Canadian and Québec recognition that made the film Dolan's international breakthrough.

The square frame is not a decorative social-media reference. It makes the three characters compete for bodily and emotional room. The rare openings of the frame matter because the production has first taught the viewer to experience width as something unavailable.
